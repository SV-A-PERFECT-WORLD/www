# Agile Manifesto, generalized

The Manifesto for Agile Software Development was written in 2001 by seventeen software practitioners. It states four values and twelve principles for making things together under uncertainty. The left-hand columns below quote the original text verbatim.[^manifesto]

I (Boran) find the agile approach so balanced and generic that it can serve many more topics.

The Generalized column takes the Original Agile Manifesto beyond software. I replaced specific or technical terms in the Original by Generalized terms so that the text applies equally to a broad range of topics, such as agriculture, boatbuilding, business or spirituality. Differences <span class="generalized">in yellow</span>.

## Values side by side

| # | Original | Generalized | notes |
|--|---|---|--|
|  | We are uncovering better ways of <span class="original">developing software</span> by doing it and helping others do it. Through this work we have come to value: | We are uncovering better ways of <span class="generalized">creating value</span> by doing it and helping others do it. Through this work we have come to value: |  |
| 1 | **Individuals and interactions** over <span class="original">processes</span> and tools | **Individuals and interactions** over <span class="generalized">methods</span> and tools |  |
| 2 | **<span class="original">Working software</span>** over comprehensive documentation | **<span class="generalized">Living systems</span>** over comprehensive documentation |  |
| 3 | **<span class="original">Customer collaboration</span>** over <span class="original">contract negotiation</span> | **<span class="generalized">Collaboration</span>** over <span class="generalized">negotiating terms and conditions</span> | _collaboration and negotiation between whatever roles (could be among peers, too)_ |
| 4 | **Responding to change** over following a plan | **Responding to change** over following a plan |  |
|  | That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more. | That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more. |  |

## Principles side by side

| # | Original | short | Generalized | notes |
|---|---|--|---|--|
| 1 | Our highest priority is to <span class="original">satisfy the customer</span> through early and continuous delivery of valuable <span class="original">software</span>. | share a yield early and continuously  | Our highest priority is to <span class="generalized">serve the receivers</span> through early and continuous delivery of valuable <span class="generalized">results</span>. |  |
| 2 | Welcome changing <span class="original">requirements</span>, even late in <span class="original">development</span>. Agile <span class="original">processes harness change for the customer's competitive advantage</span>. | welcome changing needs | Welcome changing <span class="generalized">needs</span>, even late in <span class="generalized">the creation process</span>. Agile <span class="generalized">ways turn change into surplus for the receivers</span>. |  |
| 3 | Deliver working <span class="original">software</span> frequently, from <span class="original">a couple of weeks to a couple of months</span>, with a preference to the shorter timescale. | deliver frequently | Deliver working <span class="generalized">results</span> frequently, from <span class="generalized">days or weeks to seasons or years</span>, with a preference to the shorter timescale. |  |
| 4 | <span class="original">Business people and developers</span> must work together <span class="original">daily</span> throughout the <span class="original">project</span>. | receivers and creators must work together closely  | <span class="generalized">Receivers and creators</span> must work together <span class="generalized">closely and continuously</span> throughout the <span class="generalized">endeavor</span>. | _"project" in the procedural sense: work over time_ |
| 5 | Build <span class="original">projects</span> around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done. | build systems around motivated individuals | Build <span class="generalized">systems</span> around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done. | _"project" in the structural sense: people with roles, extended to "systems", including methods and tools_ |
| 6 | The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a <span class="original">development team</span> is face-to-face conversation. | communicate directly | The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a <span class="generalized">group</span> is face-to-face conversation. | _extends to group communication over 1:1 communication, if the subject involves more than 2 people, because a group communicates more directly than a chain or hierarchy_ |
| 7 | <span class="original">Working software is</span> the primary measure of progress. | measure progress by systems and yield | <span class="generalized">Living systems and their results are</span> the primary measure of progress. |  |
| 8 | Agile <span class="original">processes</span> promote sustainable <span class="original">development</span>. <span class="original">The sponsors, developers, and users</span> should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely. | sustainable pace | Agile <span class="generalized">ways</span> promote sustainable <span class="generalized">work</span>. <span class="generalized">All involved people</span> should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely. | _also extends to other living beings and resources; while wear and tear is inevitable with some material, we prefer the more durable ones over short-lived options_ |
| 9 | Continuous attention to <span class="original">technical excellence</span> and good design enhances agility. | excellence | Continuous attention to <span class="generalized">excellence of craft</span> and good design enhances agility. |  |
| 10 | Simplicity –the art of maximizing the amount of work not done– is essential. | simplicity | Simplicity –the art of maximizing the amount of work not done– is essential. |  |
| 11 | The best <span class="original">architectures, requirements,</span> and designs emerge from self-organizing <span class="original">teams</span>. | self-organizing groups | The best <span class="generalized">structures, understanding of needs,</span> and designs emerge from self-organizing <span class="generalized">groups</span>. |  |
| 12 | At regular intervals, the <span class="original">team</span> reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly. | reflect regularly | At regular intervals, the <span class="generalized">group</span> reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly. |  |
