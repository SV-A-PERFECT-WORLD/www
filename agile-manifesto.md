# Agile Manifesto, generalized

We are uncovering better ways of creating value by doing it and helping others do it. Through this work we have come to value:

- **Individuals and interactions** over methods and tools
- **Living systems** over comprehensive documentation
- **True collaboration** over negotiating terms and conditions
- **Responding to change** over following a plan

That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more.

## Principles

1. Our highest priority is to serve the receivers through early and continuous delivery of valuable results.
2. Welcome changing needs, even late in the creation process. Agile ways turn change into surplus for the receivers.
3. Deliver working results frequently, from days or weeks to seasons or years, with a preference to the shorter timescale.
4. Receivers and creators must work together closely and continuously throughout the endeavor.
5. Build systems around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done.
6. The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a group is face-to-face conversation.
7. Living systems and their results are the primary measure of progress.
8. Agile ways promote sustainable work. All involved people should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely.
9. Continuous attention to excellence of craft and good design enhances agility.
10. Simplicity –the art of maximizing the amount of work not done– is essential.
11. The best structures, understanding of needs, and designs emerge from self-organizing groups.
12. At regular intervals, the group reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly.

*Adapted from the Manifesto for Agile Software Development (2001),[^manifesto] compared side by side with the original [below](#values-side-by-side).*

## Background

The Manifesto for Agile Software Development was written in 2001 by seventeen software practitioners. It states four values and twelve principles for making things together under uncertainty. The left-hand columns below quote the original text verbatim.[^manifesto]

I (Boran) find the agile approach so balanced and generic that it can serve many more topics.

The Generalized column takes the Original Agile Manifesto beyond software. I replaced specific or technical terms in the Original with Generalized terms so that the text applies equally to a broad range of topics, such as permaculture, sailing, boatbuilding, business or spirituality. Differences <span class="generalized">in yellow</span>.

## Related Work

Other authors came up with similar or different mappings and evolutions of the Original:

**Generalizing the original wording**

- The simplest generalization replaces "software" by "value", "systems" or "solutions"; the same article discusses turning the fourth value towards responding to feedback, and quotes Ron Jeffries, who sees the Manifesto as a historical statement rather than a living document.[^techwell]
- Allan Kelly argues that the inspect-and-adapt loop makes agile applicable anywhere, but also warns that this claim overreaches, since "agile" has no agreed definition.[^kelly]
- Research on agile outside IT is scarce. Andrew Hunt, one of the signatories, is quoted saying that agile has little to do with software: "it's all about recognizing and applying feedback".[^ieee][^bianchi]

**Domain-neutral condensations**

- Heart of Agile, by signatory Alistair Cockburn, reduces agile to four words: collaborate, deliver, reflect, improve.[^cockburn]
- Modern Agile, by Joshua Kerievsky, has four principles meant for many domains: make people awesome, make safety a prerequisite, experiment and learn rapidly, deliver value continuously.[^kerievsky]

**Rewrites for a single domain**

- Marketing: the Agile Marketing Manifesto, five value pairs in its 2020 version.[^marketing]
- Education: the Agile Manifesto for Teaching and Learning.[^teaching]
- Human resources: a Manifesto for HR Development.[^hr]
- Hardware: a reworded manifesto with a comparison table,[^altium] the 12 principles reframed for physical products,[^hajou] and the finding that 75% of the principles are not specific to software.[^tcgen]
- Church and spirituality: a book on experimentation and adaptation in church life,[^zscheile] an "agile church manifesto" with value pairs such as "relationships over processes", each tied to a Bible passage,[^seedbed] and an informal "Manifesto for Agile Religion".[^lds]
- Business: the Business Agility Manifesto shares little more than the name; it asks for business knowledge to be captured in a durable, explicit form, which runs counter to "over comprehensive documentation".[^busagility]
